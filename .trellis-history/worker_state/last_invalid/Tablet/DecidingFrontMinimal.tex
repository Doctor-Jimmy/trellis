\begin{helper}[Minimal deciding prefix extraction]
If $s$ is a deciding prefix of $h$, then some member of
$\mathsf{DecidingFrontSet}(h)$ is an initial segment of $s$.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Consider the natural numbers
\[
 \{\,\lvert t\rvert: \mathsf{InitialSegment}(t,s)\text{ and }t
 \text{ is deciding}\,\}.
\]
This set is nonempty because $t=s$ is an initial segment of itself and is
deciding by hypothesis.  Choose a realizing $t$ of its least element.

The set $t$ is deciding.  If $q$ were a proper initial segment of $t$ that
was also deciding, then $q$ would be an initial segment of $s$ by
transitivity of \noderef{InitialSegment}, and its finite cardinality would
be strictly smaller than that of $t$.  This contradicts the choice of
$t$.  Therefore $t$ is deciding and has no proper deciding initial segment,
which is exactly membership in \noderef{DecidingFrontSet}; it is also an
initial segment of $s$ by construction.
\end{proof}
