\begin{lemma}[Restriction closure]
If $F$ is a front on $X$ and $Y\subseteq X$ is infinite, then
$F\mathbin{|}Y$ is a front on $Y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Prefix-freeness is inherited from $F$.  By the operation in
\noderef{FrontRestrict}, if $Z\subseteq Y$ is infinite,
then $Z\subseteq X$ is infinite, so density of $F$ gives an $s\in F$ with
$s\segs Z$.  Since $s\subseteq Z\subseteq Y$, this member lies in
$F\mathbin{|}Y$, proving density.  The base of the restriction is contained
in $Y$ by definition.  Conversely, for each $n\in Y$, density applied to
$\{n\}\cup Y/n$ gives a member of the restriction containing $n$; hence its
base is $Y$ unless the restriction is trivial.  The exceptional case is
allowed by the first clause of \noderef{Front}.  Thus all three clauses of
the front definition hold.
\end{proof}
