\begin{helper}[Appending a larger point to the finite bridge tail]
Let $u$ be a nonempty finite set of natural numbers and let every member of
$u$ be smaller than $q$.  Then
\[
 \mathsf{FrontBridgeTail}(u\cup\{q\})
 =\mathsf{FrontBridgeTail}(u)\cup\{q\}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
By the definition of \noderef{FrontBridgeTail}, an element $k$ belongs to
the left-hand side exactly when $k\in u\cup\{q\}$ and some
$n\in u\cup\{q\}$ satisfies $n<k$.  If $k=q$, nonemptiness of $u$ gives
some $j\in u$, and the hypothesis gives $j<q$, so $q$ is in the left-hand
side.  If $k\in u$, a witness $n=q$ is impossible because $k<q$; hence
the witnesses for $k$ are exactly the witnesses from $u$.  These are
precisely the two alternatives defining the right-hand side.  Conversely,
the same argument shows that $q$ has a witness from $u$, and every witness
from $u$ remains a witness after inserting $q$.
\end{proof}
