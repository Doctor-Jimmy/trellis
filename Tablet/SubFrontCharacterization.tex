\begin{lemma}[Sub-fronts]
Let $F$ be a front on $X$ and let $F'\subseteq F$.  Then $F'$ is a front
if and only if there is an infinite $Y\subseteq X$ such that
\[
F'=F\mathbin{|}Y.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $Y\subseteq X$ is infinite, \noderef{FrontRestriction} shows that the
family defined by \noderef{FrontRestrict}, namely $F\mathbin{|}Y$, is a
front, and it is plainly contained in $F$.

Conversely, suppose $F'$ is a front on $Y$ and $F'\subseteq F$.  If $F$ is
trivial, the only possible front subfamily is the same trivial family, and
the conclusion follows by taking $Y=X$.  Otherwise $F'$ cannot contain the
empty set, so its base is $Y$ and the base clause of \noderef{Front} gives
$Y=\mathsf{FrontBase}(F')\subseteq\mathsf{FrontBase}(F)=X$.

For $s\in F'$, clearly $s\in F\mathbin{|}Y$.  Conversely, if
$s\in F\mathbin{|}Y$, density of $F'$ on $Y$ supplies $t\in F'$ with
$t\segs(s\cup(Y/s))$.  Both $s$ and $t$ belong to $F$, and one is an
initial segment of the other; prefix-freeness of $F$ forces $s=t$.  Hence
$F\mathbin{|}Y\subseteq F'$, proving equality.
\end{proof}
