\begin{helper}[Transitivity of finite initial segments]
If $a$ is an initial segment of $b$ and $b$ is an initial segment of $c$,
then $a$ is an initial segment of $c$.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Use the two alternatives in the definition of
\noderef{InitialSegment}.  Equality in either premise immediately reduces
the claim to the other premise.  In the remaining case, write
$a=\{k\in b:k<n\}$ and $b=\{k\in c:k<m\}$ with $n\in b$ and $m\in c$.
The membership $n\in b$ gives $n<m$ and $n\in c$.  Filtering $c$ first at
$m$ and then at $n$ is the same as filtering it at $n$, because
$n<m$.  Thus $a=\{k\in c:k<n\}$, which is the required initial-segment
alternative.
\end{proof}
