\begin{lemma}[All ordered one-point extensions remain in the tree]
Let $F$ be a front on the full base $\mathbb N$.  If
$s\in T(F)\setminus F$ and $n$ is such that
$\mathsf{ProperInitialSegment}(s,s\cup\{n\})$, then
$s\cup\{n\}\in T(F)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $t\in F$ with $s\segm t$ using the definition of
\noderef{PrefixTree}.  Since $t\in F$ and $s\notin F$, we have $s\ne t$.
The \noderef{InitialSegment} witness together with this inequality says
that $s$ is a proper initial segment of $t$, in the sense of
\noderef{ProperInitialSegment}.
The hypothesis on $n$, interpreted using \noderef{InitialSegment}, says
that $n$ is larger than every member of $s$.  Write $v=s\cup\{n\}$ and
consider the infinite set
\[
  Y=v\cup\{k\in\mathbb N\mid \max(v)<k\}.
\]
The set $Y$ is infinite and is contained in the full base.  Moreover, the
increasing enumeration of $Y$ begins with the increasing enumeration of $s$
and then $n$, because every element of $s$ is below $n$ and every element
of $Y\setminus v$ is above $\max(v)$.  Hence $s\segs Y$.  By density in
\noderef{Front}, choose $w\in F$ with $w\segs Y$.

Initial segments of the same increasing enumeration are linearly ordered by
inclusion.  If $w$ is no longer than $s$, then either $w=s$, contradicting
$s\notin F$, or $w$ is a proper initial segment of $s$.  In the latter case
$w$ is an initial segment of the front member $t$ chosen above.  Since both
$w$ and $t$ lie in $F$, prefix-freeness in \noderef{Front} forces $w=t$,
contradicting that $w$ is a proper initial segment of $s$ and $s$ is a
proper initial segment of $t$.  Therefore $w$ is strictly longer than $s$.
The next element after $s$ in the increasing enumeration of $Y$ is $n$, so
$n\in w$ and $v\segs w$.  By the definition of \noderef{PrefixTree},
$v\in T(F)$.
This argument is exactly the ordered-extension form of the front-density
step used at paper lines 1145--1148.
\end{proof}
