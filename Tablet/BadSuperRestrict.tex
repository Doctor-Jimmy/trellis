\begin{lemma}[Badness under sub-front restriction]
If a super-sequence is bad, then its restriction to any sub-front is bad.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take arbitrary $s,t\in F'$ with $\mathsf{FiniteShift}\ s\ t$.  Since
$F'\subseteq F$, these same previously established $s,t$ are members of
$F$, and the same finite-shift relation holds between them.  By the defining
badness statement for the original super-sequence, explicitly
\noderef{BadSuperSequence}, these $s,t\in F$ with
$\mathsf{FiniteShift}\ s\ t$ satisfy
\[
\neg r\bigl(f(s),f(t)\bigr).
\]
By \noderef{SuperSequenceRestrict}, the value of the restricted
super-sequence at $s$ (respectively, at $t$) is the value of $f$ at the same
finite set, viewed through the inclusion $F'\hookrightarrow F$.  Hence the
displayed inequality is exactly
\[
\neg r\bigl((f\!\restriction F')(s),(f\!\restriction F')(t)\bigr),
\]
which proves that the restriction is bad.
\end{proof}
