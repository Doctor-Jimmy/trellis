\begin{lemma}[Perfect base extensions yield perfect sub-super-sequences]
Let $f:F\to Q$ be a super-sequence on $X$, and let $h$ be a base
multi-sequence satisfying the value equation for the front extension.  If
$z\in\mathsf{BaseIncSeq}(X)$ and the global-coordinate restriction
$x\mapsto h(z\circ x)$ is perfect, then $f$ has a perfect restriction to a
sub-front on the exact subbase $Y=\operatorname{range}(z)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $Y=\operatorname{range}(z)$.  Since $z$ is an increasing enumeration,
$Y$ is infinite and $Y\subseteq X$.  By \noderef{FrontRestriction}, the
restriction $F\mathbin{|}Y$ is a front on $Y$ and is a subfamily of $F$.
Take $F'=F\mathbin{|}Y$.  If $s,t\in F'$ satisfy
$s\mathrel{\mathsf{FiniteShift}}t$, choose an increasing enumeration $x$ of
a subset of $Y$ witnessing this relation.  The extension equation in the
statement identifies the values of $s$ and $t$ with
$h(z\circ u)$ and $h(z\circ\mathsf{ShiftMap}(u))$ for the corresponding
enumeration $u$ of a subset of $\mathbb N$.  These are respectively the
values of $\mathsf{BaseRestrict}(h,z)$ at $u$ and at
$\mathsf{ShiftMap}(u)$.  Its perfectness gives the required relation.
Unfolding \noderef{SuperSequenceRestrict} then proves that the restricted
super-sequence on $F'$ is perfect.
\end{proof}
