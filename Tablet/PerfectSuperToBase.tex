\begin{lemma}[Super-sequence perfectness gives base perfectness]
If $f:F\to Q$ is perfect, then there is a base multi-sequence
$h:\mathsf{BaseIncSeq}(X)\to Q$ which is locally constant and perfect, and
which satisfies the extension value equation
\[
\forall x\in\mathsf{BaseIncSeq}(X)\;\exists s\in F\;\bigl(
  s\segs\operatorname{range}(x)\mathbin{\wedge}h(x)=f(s)\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The front and value map recorded by $f$ are supplied by
\noderef{SuperSequence}; write $h_F=f.\mathsf{front}$ and
$h_f=f.\mathsf{value}$.  Apply \noderef{SuperSequenceExtension} to
$F$, $X$, $h_F$, and $f$.  We obtain a base multi-sequence $h$, a proof
$h_{\mathrm{lc}}$ of $\mathsf{BaseLocallyConstant}(h)$, and the following
extension equation:
\[
 \mathsf{Ext}(y):\qquad
 \exists u\in\operatorname{Finset}(\mathbb N)\;\exists h_u:
 \mathsf{FrontPrefix}(F,u,y.1),\quad
 h(y)=h_f\!\left(\langle u,h_u.1\rangle\right)
 \tag{1}
\]
for every $y\in\mathsf{BaseIncSeq}(X)$.  Here the notation
$h_u.1$ is justified by the first conjunct in the definition of
\noderef{FrontPrefix}, and hence is a proof that $u\in F$.

We prove the perfectness clause.  Fix an arbitrary
$x\in\mathsf{BaseIncSeq}(X)$.  By the definition of
\noderef{BaseIncSeq}, $x.2$ is the range-containment statement
\[
 \operatorname{range}(x.1)\subseteq X.                         \tag{2}
\]
Apply \noderef{FrontPrefixExists} to $F$, $X$, $h_F$, $x.1$, and (2).
Choose $s_0$ and
$p_0:\mathsf{FrontPrefix}(F,s_0,x.1)$.  The shifted enumeration also has
range contained in $X$: its proof is the second component of
$\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x)$, whose definition in \noderef{BaseShift} gives
\[
 (\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x)).1=\mathsf{ShiftMap}(x.1).              \tag{3}
\]
Here we use both the composition formula in \noderef{BaseShift} and the
definition of that composition as \noderef{ShiftMap}.
Thus apply \noderef{FrontPrefixExists} again, now to
$F$, $X$, $h_F$, $(\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x)).1$, and the containment proof
$(\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x)).2$.  Choose $t_0$ and
$q_0:\mathsf{FrontPrefix}(F,t_0,(\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x)).1)$.

The equation (1), first with $y=x$, gives witnesses $s$ and
$p:\mathsf{FrontPrefix}(F,s,x.1)$ together with
\[
 e_s:\qquad h(x)=h_f\!\left(\langle s,p.1\rangle\right),             \tag{4}
\]
and, separately with $y=\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x)$, gives witnesses $t$ and
$q:\mathsf{FrontPrefix}(F,t,(\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x)).1)$ together with
\[
 e_t:\qquad h(\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x))
   =h_f\!\left(\langle t,q.1\rangle\right).                         \tag{5}
\]
The two front-prefix witnesses for $x.1$, namely $p_0$ and $p$, have the
same finite set by \noderef{FrontPrefixUnique}, so $s=s_0$.  Likewise,
applied to $q_0$ and $q$ at the shifted enumeration,
\noderef{FrontPrefixUnique} gives $t=t_0$.  We henceforth use the names
$s,t$ for these identified prefixes.  In particular, the proper-prefix
parts of $p$ and $q$ give
\[
 \mathsf{ProperPrefixSet}(s,\operatorname{range}(x.1)),qquad
 \mathsf{ProperPrefixSet}
   \bigl(t,\operatorname{range}((\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x)).1)\bigr).
 \tag{6}
\]

We now construct the exact witness required by
\noderef{FiniteShift}.  Take the increasing enumeration $x.1$ itself.
The first conjunct in the definition of $\mathsf{FiniteShift}$ is the
first assertion in (6).  For the second, use the second assertion in (6)
and rewrite its enumeration with (3); this gives
\[
 \mathsf{ProperPrefixSet}\bigl(t,
   \operatorname{range}(\mathsf{ShiftMap}(x.1))\bigr).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \mathsf{FiniteShift}(s,t)                                      \tag{7}
\]
holds with witness $x.1$, exactly because \noderef{FiniteShift} defines
this relation by those two proper-prefix conditions.

The first components $p.1$ and $q.1$ of the two prefix witnesses prove
$s\in F$ and $t\in F$.  Apply the perfectness hypothesis from the lemma
to these members and (7), using the definition in
\noderef{PerfectSuperSequence}.  We obtain
\[
 r\!\left(h_f\!\left(\langle s,p.1\rangle\right),
          h_f\!\left(\langle t,q.1\rangle\right)\right).          \tag{8}
\]
Substitute (4) and (5) into (8).  This yields
\[
 r\bigl(h(x),h(\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x))\bigr).                    \tag{9}
\]
Since $x$ was arbitrary, (9) is precisely
$\mathsf{BasePerfect}(r,h)$ by \noderef{BasePerfect}.  Together with
$h_{\mathrm{lc}}$ and the extension equation (1), this is exactly the
conclusion of the lemma.
\end{proof}
