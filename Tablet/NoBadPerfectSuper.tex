\begin{lemma}[A super-sequence is not both perfect and bad]
No super-sequence is simultaneously perfect and bad for the same relation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose an increasing enumeration of the infinite base $X$ using
\noderef{InfiniteSetEnumeration}.  By
\noderef{FrontPrefixExists}, the front has a member $s$ preceding its range
and a member $t$ preceding the shifted range.  Thus
$s\mathrel{\mathsf{FiniteShift}}t$.  Perfectness gives
$r(f(s),f(t))$, while badness gives its negation by
\noderef{PerfectSuperSequence} and \noderef{BadSuperSequence}, a
contradiction.
\end{proof}
