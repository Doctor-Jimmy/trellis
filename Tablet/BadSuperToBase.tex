\begin{lemma}[Super-sequence badness gives base badness]
If $f:F\to Q$ is a bad super-sequence on a front on $X$, then there is a
base multi-sequence $h:\mathsf{BaseIncSeq}(X)\to Q$ which is locally constant
and bad, and which satisfies the extension value equation
\[
\forall x\in\mathsf{BaseIncSeq}(X)\;\exists s\in F\;\bigl(
  s\segs\operatorname{range}(x)\mathbin{\wedge}h(x)=f(s)\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The front and value map recorded by $f$ are part of
\noderef{SuperSequence}.  Apply \noderef{SuperSequenceExtension} to them.
It supplies a base multi-sequence $h$, its local constancy, and, for every
$x\in\mathsf{BaseIncSeq}(X)$, a member $s\in F$ with
$s\segs\operatorname{range}(x)$ and $h(x)=f(s)$.

It remains to prove badness.  Fix
$x\in\mathsf{BaseIncSeq}(X)$ and apply the same extension equation to $x$
and to $\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x)$.  Let the resulting front members be $s$
and $t$.  By the definition of \noderef{FiniteShift}, the two prefix
relations, together with the shift of $x$, show
$s\mathrel{\mathsf{FiniteShift}}t$.  The hypothesis
\noderef{BadSuperSequence} therefore gives
$\neg r(f(s),f(t))$.  Substituting the two extension value equations yields
$\neg r(h(x),h(\mathsf{BaseShift}_X(x)))$.  Since $x$ was arbitrary, this is
exactly \noderef{BaseBad}.
\end{proof}
