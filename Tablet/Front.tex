\begin{definition}[Explicit front]
A family $F$ of finite subsets of $\mathbb N$ is a front on an infinite set
$X\subseteq\mathbb N$ when all of the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item either $F=\{\emptyset\}$, or $\mathsf{FrontBase}(F)=X$;
\item if $s,t\in F$ and $s$ is an initial segment of $t$, then $s=t$;
\item for every infinite $Y\subseteq X$ there is an $s\in F$ with
      $s\segs Y$.
\end{enumerate}
The trivial front $\{\emptyset\}$ is therefore a front on every infinite
base, exactly as in the paper.
\end{definition}
