\begin{theorem}[g-BQO]
For every nonidentity $g\in\mathsf{IncSeq}$ and every binary relation $R$
on a discrete type $A$,
\[
(A,R)\text{ is a $g$-better-relation}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(A,R)\text{ is a better-relation}.
\]
In particular, if $Q$ is a quasi-order with relation $\leq$, then
\[
Q\text{ is $g$-bqo}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Q\text{ is bqo}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $g\neq\mathsf{IncSeqId}$.  By \noderef{MainProp},
\noderef{ContMorEq}, and the definition of \noderef{ContMor}, choose
continuous homomorphisms
\[
 K:(\mathsf{IncSeq},\mathsf{RightComp}(g))\longrightarrow
       (\mathsf{IncSeq},\mathsf{ShiftMap}),
 \qquad
 L:(\mathsf{IncSeq},\mathsf{ShiftMap})\longrightarrow
       (\mathsf{IncSeq},\mathsf{RightComp}(g)).
\]
Thus, by the two fields of \noderef{ContinuousHom}, $K$ and $L$ are
prefix-continuous and satisfy
\[
 K(\mathsf{RightComp}(g)(x))=\mathsf{ShiftMap}(K(x)),
 \qquad
 L(\mathsf{ShiftMap}(x))=\mathsf{RightComp}(g)(L(x)).
\]

We spell out how these maps transport complement homomorphisms.  Let
$H:(\mathsf{IncSeq},\mathsf{ShiftMap})\to(A,R^{\complement})$ be a
continuous homomorphism in the sense of \noderef{ContinuousRelHom}.  Define
$J(x)=H(K(x))$.  To check local constancy at $x$, first use local constancy
of $H$ to choose an output length $m$ such that every $z$ agreeing with
$K(x)$ through $m$ has $H(z)=H(K(x))$.  Prefix-continuity of $K$ then gives
an input length $k$ such that agreement with $x$ through $k$ forces
$K(z)$ to agree with $K(x)$ through $m$.  Consequently $J(z)=J(x)$ for
all such $z$.  For the homomorphism equation, use the first displayed
commutation equation and then the homomorphism field of $H$:
\[
 R^{\complement}(J(x),J(\mathsf{RightComp}(g)(x)))
 =R^{\complement}(H(K(x)),H(\mathsf{ShiftMap}(K(x)))) .
\]
Thus $J$ is a complement homomorphism for $\mathsf{RightComp}(g)$.  The
same argument with $L$ in place of $K$ transports a complement homomorphism
in the reverse direction.  Therefore the absence of the complement
homomorphism in the second clause of \noderef{GBetterRel} is equivalent to
the absence in \noderef{BetterRel}; the source actions are precisely those
of \noderef{RightComp} and \noderef{ShiftMap}.

It remains to justify, in the detail needed for the first clause of
\noderef{GBetterRel}, why absence of an $R$-homomorphic restriction is the
same as the existence of a complement homomorphism.  Let
$\varphi:\mathsf{IncSeq}\to A$ be locally constant and put
\[
 E=\{y\in\mathsf{IncSeq}\mid
       R(\varphi(y),\varphi(\mathsf{RightComp}(g)(y)))\}.
\]
The set $E$ is open in the finite-prefix topology.  Indeed, local constancy
of $\varphi$ decides the first value at $y$, and local constancy of the
second value is obtained as follows: the first $n$ values of
$\mathsf{RightComp}(g)(y)=y\circ g$ depend only on the first $g(n)$ values
of $y$.  Hence a sufficiently long prefix of $y$ decides both values in
the definition of $E$.

Assume first that the first clause fails for this $\varphi$.  Thus, for
every increasing enumeration $f$, some $y$ satisfies
\[
 \neg R(\varphi(f\circ y),\varphi(f\circ y\circ g)).
 \tag{1}
\]
We now give the finite-prefix fusion which turns these witnesses into one
witness working for every input.  Call a finite increasing prefix $s$
deciding if every increasing sequence extending $s$ belongs to $E$.  Since
$E$ is open, every member of $E$ has a finite deciding prefix.  For a finite
prefix $s$ and an infinite reservoir $X$ above its last entry, call $(s,X)$
positive if every infinite increasing sequence whose prefix is $s$ and whose
remaining entries lie in $X$ has a further finite deciding prefix; call it
negative if no infinite subreservoir of $X$ makes $(s,\mathord{-})$
positive.  The failure in (1) says exactly that the empty prefix is negative
on the full set of natural numbers: a positive reservoir would enumerate an
$f$ for which the relation in (1) never fails.

We maintain the stronger fusion invariant that every finite prefix formed
from the entries already chosen is negative on the current reservoir.  At
stage $n$, list the finitely many such prefixes and list the finitely many
input prefixes $t_0,\ldots,t_n$ that have been considered.  For each of
these finitely many cases, negativity says that an infinite subreservoir
still has a continuation witnessing failure of the $R$-relation.  Local
constancy of $\varphi$ and of $\varphi$ after right composition by $g$
turns that witness into a finite decision: only finitely many outer
coordinates are queried.  Choose the next outer entry above all queried
coordinates and shrink the reservoir above it, successively for this finite
list.  If this finite choice were impossible for some prefix $s$, then every
possible next entry would have a subreservoir on which $s$ was positive.
Choosing the next entry and then the corresponding subreservoir recursively
would give an infinite reservoir on which the preceding prefix was positive,
contradicting its negativity.  Thus we obtain nested finite prefixes
$u_0\subset u_1\subset\cdots$ and infinite reservoirs
$X_{n+1}\subseteq X_n$ above $u_{n+1}$, and every decision made for one of
the $t_i$ is preserved by all later stages.

Let $x=\bigcup_n u_n$.  The prefixes are nested and the reservoirs are
infinite and lie above the preceding prefix, so $x$ is an increasing
enumeration.  Fix any $y\in\mathsf{IncSeq}$.  Some $t_i$ is a prefix of $y$,
and at a later stage all outer coordinates queried by the local-constancy
decision for $x\circ y$ and $x\circ y\circ g$ have entered the fusion.
The corresponding finite prefix $x\circ t_i$ is among the prefixes covered
by the negative invariant, so it has no deciding extension inside the chosen
reservoir.  The invariant then gives
\[
 \neg R(\varphi(x\circ y),
        \varphi(x\circ y\circ g))
 \quad\text{for every }y. \tag{2}
\]
Define $H(y)=\varphi(x\circ y)$.  This restriction is locally constant:
at $y$, choose from local constancy of $\varphi$ a finite output prefix of
$x\circ y$ deciding $\varphi(x\circ y)$; the finitely many values of $y$
needed to select those entries of $x$ form an input prefix, and every
extension agreeing with that prefix has the same $H$-value.  Equation (2),
with the definition of \noderef{RightComp}, is exactly
\[
 R^{\complement}(H(y),H(\mathsf{RightComp}(g)(y))).
\]
Together with local constancy, \noderef{ContinuousRelHom} shows that $H$
is a complement homomorphism.  This contradicts the second clause.

Conversely, suppose that $H:(\mathsf{IncSeq},\mathsf{RightComp}(g))
\to(A,R^{\complement})$ is a complement homomorphism.  For every $f$,
define its restriction by
\[
 H_f(y)=H(f\circ y).
\]
Local constancy of $H_f$ follows directly from that of $H$: a finite prefix
of $f\circ y$ deciding $H(f\circ y)$ is determined by a finite prefix of
$y$.  Associativity of composition and the definition of
\noderef{RightComp} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R^{\complement}(H_f(y),H_f(\mathsf{RightComp}(g)(y)))
 &=R^{\complement}(H(f\circ y),H((f\circ y)\circ g))\\
 &=R^{\complement}(H(f\circ y),H(\mathsf{RightComp}(g)(f\circ y))).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus every $H_f$ is a complement homomorphism.  It cannot at the same time
be an $R$-homomorphism, since at any input the two homomorphism equations
would assert both $R(a,b)$ and $R^{\complement}(a,b)$, where the latter is
by definition $\neg R(a,b)$.  Therefore no restriction in the first clause
can exist.  We have proved the equivalence of the two clauses in
\noderef{GBetterRel}.  Combining it with the transport of the second clause
to the no-complement formulation in \noderef{BetterRel} proves the first
displayed equivalence.

Finally let $r$ be a quasi-order on $Q$.  By \noderef{BetterRel}, the
ordinary better-relation condition is the nonexistence of a
\noderef{ContinuousRelHom} from the shift into the complement relation.
Unpacking such a structure gives a locally constant map
$h:\mathsf{IncSeq}\to Q$ and, for every $x$,
\[
 \neg r(h(x),h(\mathsf{ShiftMap}(x))).
\]
By \noderef{BadMultiSequence}, this last condition says exactly that $h$
is bad for $r$.  Conversely, a locally constant bad multi-sequence supplies
the two fields of a \noderef{ContinuousRelHom} into the complement
relation.  Therefore the absence of this homomorphism is equivalent to the
statement in \noderef{Bqo} that every locally constant $h$ is not bad.
Thus $\operatorname{BetterRel}(r)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Bqo}(r)$,
and the already proved general equivalence yields
\[
 \operatorname{GBetterRel}(g,r)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Bqo}(r).
\]
\end{proof}
