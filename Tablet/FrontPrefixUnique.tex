\begin{lemma}[Front member uniqueness]
Let $F$ be a family of finite subsets which is a front on $X$, let $x$ be
an increasing enumeration, and let $s,t$ be finite subsets such that
$\mathsf{FrontPrefix}(F,s,x)$ and
$\mathsf{FrontPrefix}(F,t,x)$.
Then $s=t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \noderef{FrontPrefix}, both hypotheses say that $s,t\in F$ and that
each is a proper prefix of $\operatorname{range}(x)$.  Hence the increasing
enumeration of one of $s,t$ is an initial segment of the increasing
enumeration of the other, so either
$\mathsf{InitialSegment}(s,t)$ or
$\mathsf{InitialSegment}(t,s)$ holds.  The prefix-free clause in the front
hypothesis \noderef{Front} then forces $s=t$.
\end{proof}
