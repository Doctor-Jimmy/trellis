\begin{lemma}[Increasing enumeration of an infinite set]
Every infinite $X\subseteq\mathbb N$ has a strictly increasing enumeration
$x\in\mathsf{IncSeq}$ whose range is exactly $X$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Define $x(0)$ to be the least element of $X$, and recursively let $x(n+1)$
be the least member of $X$ greater than $x(n)$.  Infinitude of $X$ ensures
that each recursive choice exists.  The resulting map is strictly
increasing, hence an element of $\mathsf{IncSeq}$, and every value lies in
$X$.  Conversely, if $n\in X$, the finite set of members of $X$ below $n$
has a finite cardinality, so the recursion reaches $n$ at that index.
Thus the range is $X$.
\end{proof}
