\begin{lemma}[Perfect-or-bad shift dichotomy]
For every relation $r$ and every locally constant multi-sequence $h$, there
is an infinite restriction on which $h$ is either perfect or bad.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Form the minimal deciding front $F^h$ using
\noderef{DecidingFront}; its value map exists by
\noderef{DecidingValue}.  Color $s\in F^h$ according to whether its value
is related by $r$ to the value at a finite shift of $s$.  This is a binary
coloring of a front.  Apply \noderef{NashWilliams} to obtain an infinite
sub-front on which the color is constant.  If the constant color is the
positive color, the induced restriction satisfies
\noderef{PerfectMultiSequence}; if it is the negative color, the same
finite-shift calculation gives \noderef{BadMultiSequence}.  Reindexing the
infinite subbase by an increasing embedding gives the displayed $z$, and
\noderef{MultiSequenceRestrict} identifies the resulting sequence with the
restriction of $h$.
\end{proof}
