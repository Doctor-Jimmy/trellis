\begin{lemma}
Assume $k<g(k)$ and put $G(n)=g^{[n]}(k)$.  Every $l\in\mathbb N$ either
satisfies $l<G(0)$ or belongs to exactly one orbit block
$[G(n),G(n+1))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $l<G(0)$ there is nothing to prove.  Otherwise, by
\noderef{OrbitUnbounded}, choose an $m$ with $l<G(m+1)$.  The least such
index $n$ has $l<G(n+1)$, while minimality and $l\geq G(0)$ imply
$G(n)\leq l$; hence $l$ lies in the $n$th block.  If two indices $n<m$ both
worked, strict increase of the orbit from \noderef{OrbitEmbedding} would give
$G(m)\geq G(n+1)>l$, contradicting the lower-bound clause for the $m$th
block.  Thus the index is unique.
\end{proof}
